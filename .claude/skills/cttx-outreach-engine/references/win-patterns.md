# Win patterns — how CTTX deals actually advance

Source: the CTTX mailbox, 25 Sep – 2 Oct 2026. These are the moves that turned cold emails into a
survey (Kwandwe) and a promised order number (MTO Forestry). Every reply the engine handles is
checked against them. Calibration, not templates: the moves repeat, the facts never do.

## Pattern 1 — "We already have X" is the buying signal (MTO, Buffalo Kloof)
**What happened.** MTO South replied to the cold email: *"We recently completed an installation of
17 digital detection cameras… all towers are line of sight… one centralised control room."*
It read like a brush-off. It was the opening: the group ITC manager (Stephen) had had the idea
years ago — use their own towers as a network — but didn't know how. Buffalo Kloof's reply
(*"we already have a LoRa network with 27 towers"*) is the same signal.

**The move.**
1. Treat "we already have towers / cameras / LoRa / radios / fibre at one site" as an asset inventory,
   never an objection.
2. Ask for their own data in a named format: *KMZ of the towers + Google Maps pins of the offices/camps
   + where the fibre lands*. People who built towers have this file.
3. Reframe in one sentence: *"Your towers already see each other — all that's missing is the radio
   links between them. It works like a LAN extension of the fibre you already pay for."*

**The smart discovery to hunt for (research dimension in every Full discovery):**
existing vertical infrastructure built for another purpose — fire-detection camera masts,
LoRa/collar gateways, CCTV masts, radio repeaters, met masts, water towers, silos — **plus** one site
with fibre or a carrier link, **plus** several remote offices each paying for their own connectivity.
That combination is an owned backbone where only the radios are missing.

## Pattern 2 — Speed with engineering on THEIR data (MTO)
**What happened.** The KMZ arrived 30 Sep 15:05. At 16:17 Gerhard sent a hop-by-hop plan on MTO's own
towers (4 hops, 82 km, terrain clear), the proposal and a 7-sheet drawing set. Next morning the
client widened the scope to every office from Longmore to Knysna and sent tower specs, the access
contact, and an offer of help from their tower installer.

**The move.** When a client sends their own data, turn it around the **same day**, on their map,
with real engineering (use `cttx-link-assessment-architect` with their towers as candidate nodes).
Same-day engineering on the client's own data beats every pitch.

## Pattern 3 — Show the saving beyond the ask (MTO)
**What happened.** Stephen asked about two problem plantations. The reply showed that the backbone
also passes Kruisfontein, Kranshoek and Covie, and runs within one hop of six more sites, *"any of which
can hop on and cancel the connectivity service it pays for today… send me what each site pays and
I'll show the saving site by site next to the build cost."* He widened the scope himself.

**The move.** Always answer the question asked, then map every other site the backbone passes and
ask for current per-site spend. That turns a two-site fix into a network-wide business case
(Infrastructure Ownership lens) and gives CTTX the numbers for the ROI.

## Pattern 4 — "Infrastructure company, not a service provider" (MTO)
Say it plainly when a carrier/ISP comparison is in the air: *"We don't sell you bandwidth, and we
don't meter, throttle or cap these links. It's your own fibre, extended to your own sites. Our job is
to build it to carry whatever you put on it, and to maintain and support it under contract."*
(This is the Layer 1/2 + Bridge Analogy position in one paragraph.)

## Pattern 5 — Their map, their champion (Kwandwe)
**What happened.** Angus said yes → Gerhard followed up with a terrain study *"so the meeting starts
from your own map"* → Angus delegated to Gary (TFT Security, the security operator) → date set for
Tue 6 Oct.

**The move.** After a yes, deepen the free desk study onto their property before the meeting. Expect
and welcome the delegate: the security contractor / ITC manager / ops manager becomes the champion.
Give that person exactly what they need to look good internally.

## Pattern 6 — Procurement is part of the sale (MTO)
**What happened.** After agreement came: vendor-registration form, documents, a quotation for the
survey fee, then the PO. The client chased the quote ("I received no quote from Traci-Lee"), and an
internal gap surfaced (no COIDA Letter of Good Standing).

**The move.** Send the survey quotation **with** the proposal, not after. Keep a vendor pack ready
(CIPC registration, B-BBEE certificate/affidavit, SARS tax PIN, bank confirmation letter, COIDA Letter
of Good Standing). Missing paperwork can stall a won deal for longer than the selling took.

## Pattern 7 — Wording that confuses (Jeffreys Bay)
Otto replied: *"I am confused. Who roped you in for the study?"* A site manager assumes that a
"study" was commissioned by someone above him. Say it plainly: *"I did this on my own initiative,
from public terrain data — nobody commissioned it."*

## How the engine applies this
- **Reply desk (Step 0):** classify every reply as one of: asset inventory (P1) · yes → deepen on their
  map (P5) · data received → same-day engineering (P2) · procurement step (P6) · confusion (P7) ·
  not now / no. Draft the matching move.
- **Discovery (Step 2):** always research the asset inventory dimension above (installer case studies,
  tenders, press releases, satellite imagery of masts, the client's own posts).
- **Prospecting:** installers of tower networks (fire detection, LoRa/collar systems, CCTV) are a
  channel. Their reference lists are lists of properties that already have masts and a control room,
  where an owned backbone is mostly radios away.
- **Measure:** time from client data received → engineering returned (target: same day).
