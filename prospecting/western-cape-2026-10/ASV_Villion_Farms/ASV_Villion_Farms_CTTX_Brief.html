The approach

Your own private backbone, on a carrier-grade link

We bring a dedicated carrier link to your central packhouse at Werda. From there, Villion owns a private network that runs along the valley to each Hex unit and to Nuy. Its relays sit on high ground with their own solar and battery power, so the network stays up when the grid does not. River levels, pumps, cold rooms, generators, cameras and voice all run on that one backbone. CTTX supports it under SLA.

Early warning, not hindsight

River-level and rain sensors upstream and at your farms send alerts to managers' phones. That gives you time to shut down or lift pumps, move equipment and close crossings before the water arrives, and to make sure the people living near the river are safe.

Pumps and water without the drive

Pump state, pressure, flow and power are visible from the office. Faults show up as they happen, not on the next round, and water use is on record.

A cold chain you can prove

Cold-room temperatures, generator run-hours and fuel levels stay visible through a grid outage. You get the temperature record your buyers ask for, and diesel you can manage.

Owned, not rented

The network is built into the rebuild and belongs to Villion. It is not a separate service at each unit that has to be replaced after every flood. CTTX supports it under SLA.

Proof that fits: Sandy Mountain, a 33,000 ha off-grid reserve

The reserve went from a 3/8 Mbps consumer service to 100 Mbps symmetrical carrier backhaul. It now has an 800+ Mbps backbone across the property on off-grid solar relays with battery telemetry, and call-outs have dropped to zero.
