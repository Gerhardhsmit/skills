## 2. How this business makes and loses money

**Value creation**
- FACT: ASV is a vertically integrated grower–packer–exporter. It markets its own fruit, including into Asia. Its Asian strategy is differentiation by colour, size and flavour. Source: asiafruitchina.net/17327.html.

**Time-critical window**
- FACT: The Hex River is the last South African region to harvest, from about late December to mid-April. Source: southafrica.co.za Hex Valley production page (search summary).
- FACT: Anton Viljoen Jr said Peru now affects the start of the season and India sells growing volumes in the Hex's traditional late window. He said the Hex marketing window has shrunk **from 25 to 12 weeks over 11 years**, which is "why it has become important to have modern packhouses that can pack and ship the crop as soon as possible". Source: Fruitnet "New windows force Hex hands" (c. 2019).
- INTERPRETATION: Speed from vine to cold room to container is a margin lever this business has invested in directly.

## 3. Niche economics

- FACT: Hex River Valley is surrounded by **200+ table grape farms** and lies 35 km north of Worcester on the N1. Source: https://www.sa-venues.com/attractionswc/de-doorns.php
- FACT: **Hexkoel** in De Doorns precools, stores and dispatches about 15 million cartons a season for 80+ exporters and 250 packhouses from December to April. Source: http://www.hexkoel.co.za/
  - Whether Villion uses Hexkoel or relies only on its own precooling: UNKNOWN.
- FACT: Research shows that temperature breaks in the SA–UK grape cold chain are worst between leaving cold storage and the reefer being reconnected at port. Loads more than 2 h on the road need reefer gensets or refrigerated trucks. Sources:
  - https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352146522007633
  - https://www.researchgate.net/publication/378740373
- FACT: About half of SA grape exports go to the EU and about 25% to the UK. Source: search summary of FreshPlaza/CBI.
- **May 2026 floods (factual, valley-level):**
  - The storm ran from 10 to 12 May 2026. About 200 mm fell in 48 h in a valley that averages about 330 mm a year. Sources: Daily Maverick 28 Jul 2026 (https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-07-28-grapes-of-wrath-cape-farmers-face-a-long-post-flood-road-to-recovery/) and FreshPlaza.
    - Caution: the "200 mm / 330 mm / eight bridges / R250m" figures also appear in an older Fruitnet piece (https://www.fruitnet.com/fresh-produce-journal/hex-river-valley-flooded-out/147082.article, likely a previous flood). **Attribute them to 2026 only after checking the DM page.**
  - One Hex grower measured 250 mm in a month and called it "the most water we've ever seen in the Hex River Valley". Bridge damage meant growers could not reach Cape Town, Wolseley or Worcester from the valley. Source: https://www.freshplaza.com/north-america/article/9837829/most-water-we-ve-ever-seen-in-the-hex-river-valley/
  - SATI estimated **100–120 ha affected in the Hex River region**. Damage included collapsed netting and poles, trellising and irrigation. Some farms had more than a third of plantings affected. "A major concern at present is access", with roads washed away and some farms inaccessible. Sources:
    - https://www.businessday.co.za/economy/2026-05-19-table-grape-producers-plead-for-government-help-after-flood-devastation/
    - https://www.satgi.co.za/sati-calls-for-urgent-disaster-relief-following-severe-storm-damage-in-western-cape-table-grape-regions/
  - Establishing 1 ha of table grapes can cost **up to R1.2 million**. Source: SATI, same.
  - This was the **third severe flood in five years**. Vines replanted in August 2025 after the 2024 floods were washed away again. Sources: SATI; https://www.freshplaza.com/north-america/article/9841900/
  - The event has been classified as a **provincial disaster**. Source: SATI "Continued Support", June 2026.
  - Eskom reported **major faults affecting De Doorns, Touwsrivier and surrounding farms**, and some infrastructure was unreachable because of flooding. Restoration reached about 85% by late May. Sources:
    - https://www.citizen.co.za/news/severe-weather-continues-disrupt-electricity-western-cape-areas-affected/
    - https://novanews.co.za/paarlpost/eskom-restores-85-of-power-outages-after-devastating-floods/
  - The **N1 was closed for about four days** from 11 May, with Worcester cut off on the N1, R43, R46 and R60. Sources:
    - https://novanews.co.za/n1-reopens-as-western-cape-continues-flood-recovery-efforts/
    - https://agrinews.co.za/western-cape-disaster-agricultural-heartland/
  - Farm worker homes were destroyed on a neighbouring De Doorns farm (Kleinberg Kriegler, not Villion). Residents were evacuated at night. Source: https://groundup.org.za/article/de-doorns-farm-workers-homes-destroyed-in-floods/
  - Five to six people died across the province. Mention this in the internal record only; it does not belong in outreach.
- **Did communications fail?**
  - FACT, regional: one Wolseley report describes a person "without network connection due to power outages since 2am". Source: allafrica.com/stories/202605130013.html (GroundUp).
  - No source was found that specifically documents mobile or internet failure in the Hex River Valley or at Villion. That is **UNKNOWN**.
  - INTERPRETATION: Eskom faults in De Doorns and on surrounding farms lasted days in places. Mobile sites and wireless relays depend on mains power and battery backup, so loss of communications on farms during the outage is plausible. It is a question to ask, not a claim to make.
- Early warning: the DWS operates hydrological gauging on the Hex River (e.g. station H2R001, https://www.dws.gov.za/hydrology/Verified/HyDataSets.aspx?Station=H2R001). Roode Elsberg Dam is upstream on the Sanddrif, about 10 km NW of De Doorns. The DM account says a "large upstream dam … overflowed after about 18 hours of rain".
  - Whether farms receive real-time river-level alerts: UNKNOWN.
  - SATI publicly calls for "long-term planning and climate resilience measures".

**Resilience**
- FACT: Eskom's distribution faults hit De Doorns and surrounding farms in May 2026.
- INTERPRETATION: any link without independent power at its relay points will drop when the grid does.

**Terrain (INTERPRETATION)**
- This is a narrow valley bounded by the Hex River and Kwadouw mountains, with farms along the floor. Long, clear sightlines along the valley axis are likely. Side valleys and Nuy will need relays.
- High sites on the valley flanks are probable. CTTX mast dataset and LOS screening not yet run. That is the next action.

**How CTTX would differ for this client**
- A dedicated carrier-grade link would land at the central packhouse, the place where the most value moves in the shortest time.
- From there, Villion would own a private backbone along the valley. It would join the Hex units and Nuy, with solar/battery-backed relays so it keeps running when the grid does not.
- The same network would carry pumps, river-level sensors, cold-room and generator telemetry, CCTV and voice.
