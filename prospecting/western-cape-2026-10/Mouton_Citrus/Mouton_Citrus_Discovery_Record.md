## 2. How this business makes and loses money

- **Revenue:** export fruit, overwhelmingly to the US. FACT: import records show about 99% of shipments go to the US: https://www.importyeti.com/supplier/mouton-citrus (screen record). They are one of the leading growers in the "Summer Citrus from South Africa" (SCSA) programme that supplies the US during the northern summer; Boet Mouton chairs the SCSA board. FACT: https://www.freshfruitportal.com/news/2024/08/30/summer-citrus-up-close-personal-with-boet-mouton/
  - INTERPRETATION: the 99% figure reflects US import records only (other markets don't appear in US customs data). It shows the US programme is central, not that other markets are zero. Website says they target the Americas, Europe and Asia. GF Marketing also lists them as a grower (Middle East/Asia markets). FACT: https://gfmarketing.co.za/growers/
- **Value proposition in their own words:** "the important part is to be your reliable, high-quality, consistent citrus supplier for the complete summer season"; working together "takes a lot of risk out of the total supply chain to ensure fresh fruit is available weekly". FACT (Boet Mouton, Aug 2024): freshfruitportal link above.
  - INTERPRETATION: their margin rests on **weekly reliability** into a programme. A missed packing day or a lost shipping slot is not just lost volume; it is a gap in a weekly programme commitment that Boet Mouton personally chairs.
- **Harvest window (their own calendar):** Satsumas March–April; Nova May–June; Cara Cara May–June; Orri June–July; Nadorcott July–August; lemons and late oranges through the season. FACT: https://moutoncitrus.co.za/our-products/easy-peelers/ ; https://moutoncitrus.co.za/our-products/oranges/ (search summaries). INTERPRETATION: roughly March to September is packhouse peak; the US programme ships through the northern summer.
- **Cost drivers:** seasonal labour (about 2,000), packhouse energy (partly solar), cold storage, transport 170–200 km to Cape Town port, compliance. Magnitudes UNKNOWN.
- **Compliance they must evidence:**
  - Fairtrade (FLO) on all own production; Ecocert organic for rooibos. FACT (screen record, moutoncitrus.co.za search summary).
  - US market access: only production units in declared citrus black spot (CBS) free areas of the Western Cape (and a few other districts) may export to the US; units must be registered; fruit passes an APHIS preclearance programme; and fruit is cold-treated in transit against false codling moth (schedule cut from 24 to 22 days). FACT: USDA FAS Citrus Annual 2025, https://apps.fas.usda.gov/newgainapi/api/Report/DownloadReportByFileName?fileName=Citrus+Annual_Pretoria_South+Africa+-+Republic+of_SF2025-0042.pdf ; https://www.nda.agric.za/doaDev/sideMenu/plantHealth/exportsProg.htm
  - INTERPRETATION: that means orchard-to-pallet traceability per registered production unit (the nine FBO units above) and a cold chain that must be right before the container leaves. Where APHIS preclearance inspection physically happens for Mouton fruit (own packhouse or a central facility): UNKNOWN.
- **Where they lose money (evidenced):**
  - **Floods cutting the valley.** June 2023: Citrusdal cut off after the Olifants River washed away the N7 access; orchards along the river under water; "major infrastructure damage, like road and pump stations". ## 3. Niche economics

- SA citrus exported a record ~203 million 15 kg cartons (~3.05 Mt) in 2025. FACT: https://www.ecofinagency.com/news-agriculture/1111-50366-south-africa-s-citrus-exports-hit-record-3mln-tons-in-2025
- US is a niche, premium, protocol-bound market for Western Cape growers: only CBS-free areas qualify, with preclearance and in-transit cold treatment. Reduction from 24 to 22 days cut shipping cost and cold-damage loss. FACT: USDA FAS Citrus Annual 2025 (above).
- About 35,000 jobs connected to SA citrus exports to the US; Citrusdal described as a town where US exports are "the economic heart". FACT: https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/citrus-industry-welcomes-ustariff-exemption-on-oranges-2025-11-17 ; https://www.freightnews.co.za/article/citrusdal-faces-a-bleak-future-without-us-citrus-benefits
- Tariff path: 30% from Aug 2025 → oranges exempt Nov 2025 → mandarins still 30% for the 2026 season. FACT (above).
- Weather shocks: three flood events in four seasons cutting road access to Citrusdal (2023, 2024, 2026). FACT (above).
- INTERPRETATION: in this niche, the owner's worries are (1) access to the US on acceptable terms, (2) getting every week's volume packed, cooled, inspected and out of the valley, and (3) cost per carton on tariffed lines. Connectivity matters only where it touches (2) and (3).
