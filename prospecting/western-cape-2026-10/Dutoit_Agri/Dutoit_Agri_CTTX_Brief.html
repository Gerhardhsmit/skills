The approach

Your own private backbone, alongside the links you have

You have already brought proper connectivity to your farms and packhouses. We would add what sits inside: a Dutoit-owned network across each production cluster, starting with the Koue Bokkeveld farms, Ceres head office, packhouses and cold stores. It runs on its own solar-and-battery relay sites, so it does not depend on the grid. One backbone carries pumps, sensors, cold-room alarms, cameras and voice, and CTTX supports it under SLA.

Every store visible from Ceres

Room temperature, O2/CO2, compressor and generator status reach your team on a network that keeps running through an outage.

Pumps and dams without the drive

Live status, flow and dam levels across the valleys. Measured water data for buyer stewardship reporting, not estimates.

Block-level climate data

Weather stations and sensors in the orchards, live, for hour-scale heat and hail decisions.

Owned, not rented

The backbone is Dutoit's asset and fits a standardise-simplify-automate ICT strategy. Optional second carrier path into Ceres for resilience. Supported by CTTX under SLA.

Proof that fits: Sandy Mountain, 33,000 ha, off-grid

We moved the reserve from a 3/8 Mbps consumer service to 100 Mbps symmetrical carrier backhaul and an 800+ Mbps private backbone. Off-grid solar relays report their own battery state. Call-outs went to zero.

One honest point: we have not seen inside your network, and you may already have parts of this in place. The first conversation is to find out where it reaches and where it doesn't.
